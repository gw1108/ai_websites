# Araba's Shea Store — D2C Website

## Purpose

This project exists to **visualize and iterate on a direct-to-consumer (D2C) website for Araba's Shea** — a small, purely-online Amazon business creating health and beauty care products from Ghana.

Current storefront: [Araba's Shea on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/stores/ArabasShea/page/DFD14977-FFA0-47B3-BEB5-BDD6D15E4E22)

The brand today has a small catalog (~4 SKUs), all with raw/natural/handmade positioning and a strong Ghana origin story:

- Raw Batana Oil for hair growth (2oz and 4oz)
- Traditional African Black Soap — 100% raw, handmade in Ghana, with shea butter & coconut oil
- Raw Shea Butter — 100% pure, unrefined African shea butter for hair and body

Fulfillment stays on Amazon for now, so the site should feature prominent "Buy on Amazon" CTAs while owning the brand story, photography, and customer relationship.

## Design Synthesis

The design brief, compressed into one line:

> **Satin Naturel's clean beauty layout + Oishii's origin-story hero + 100% Pure's ingredient-first messaging + Ocelot Chocolate's founder/craft narrative, executed at Seven Grams Caffé's small-catalog scale.**

Concretely:

- **Warm earth tones** — shea gold, soap black, deep greens
- **Large, beautiful product photography** — butter texture, soap lather, oil sheen — with the design receding behind it
- **A Ghana sourcing/origin section** — imagery of shea nuts, traditional processing, and Ghanaian craft as the emotional hook
- **Ingredient-first messaging** — 100% raw, unrefined, handmade as the main selling proposition; organize by concern (acne, dark spots, dry skin, hair growth) as well as by product
- **Small-catalog simplicity** — no mega-menus; a clean one-page-feel store at the scale of ~4 products
- **Prominent "Buy on Amazon" CTAs** immediately after the emotional hooks

## Inspiration Sites (Top 7)

Selected from Site Builder Report's [50+ D2C website examples](https://www.sitebuilderreport.com/inspiration/d2c-websites), ranked by relevance to a natural skincare/haircare brand with a small catalog and an origin story.

### 1. Satin Naturel — https://satinnaturel.com/

The closest direct match: a natural beauty brand built on big, beautiful product photography and generous whitespace. This is the baseline template for Araba's — let warm photos of raw shea butter and black soap carry the page.

### 2. 100% Pure — https://www.100percentpure.com/

A natural skincare brand whose entire identity is ingredient transparency ("colors from fruit, infused with green tea"). Araba's has the same story to tell — 100% raw shea, unrefined batana, handmade black soap. Borrow how they lead with ingredients and purity claims, and how they organize a beauty catalog by skin/hair concern as well as by product.

### 3. Oishii — https://www.oishii.com/

The masterclass in origin-story premium positioning. They sell a Japanese omakase berry using a full-screen hero video that makes you want the product, immediately followed by the shop CTA. Araba's Ghana sourcing story is exactly this kind of asset — it instantly differentiates the brand from generic Amazon-listing competitors.

### 4. Ocelot Chocolate — https://ocelotchocolate.com/

The best small-artisan parallel: a couple making organic chocolate from their apartment, grown into a worldwide brand on the strength of a founder story and craft aesthetic. Araba's is the same shape of business — a small handmade brand where "who makes this and why" is the moat. Study how they weave the founders into the shopping experience without it feeling like a separate About page.

### 5. Seven Grams Caffé — https://sevengramscaffe.com/

The practical model for a small catalog: ultra-minimalist design that stays out of the way and puts mouth-watering product photography in the spotlight. With only ~4 products, Araba's doesn't need complex navigation — a clean one-page-feel store with a few gorgeous close-up shots is the right scale.

### 6. Sodashi — https://sodashi.ca/

A representative online beauty store whose navigation drops into many individual product pages targeting popular search queries. Worth studying for how beauty stores structure product pages for SEO — important for an Amazon-only brand building web presence from zero.

### 7. Karmin Hair Tools — https://karminhairtools.com/

A sleek, user-friendly hair-category store with easy navigation and personalization (currency/language switching). Useful reference for how the hair care side of the catalog can be navigated and presented.
